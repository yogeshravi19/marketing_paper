## PART B: FULL ACADEMIC LITERATURE REVIEW (Section 2 for Research Manuscript)

### 2. Literature Review

#### 2.1 Digital Marketing in Small Businesses and the Customer Acquisition Paradox
Small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) increasingly rely on digital channels—particularly social media platforms, search engines, and instant messaging applications—to compete for new customers under severe financial and technical constraints (Ahmad et al., 2019; Roosdhani et al., 2023; De, 2025). However, empirical findings on whether digital marketing actually improves SME performance remain sharply divided. Drawing on the Technology–Organization–Environment (TOE) and Diffusion of Innovations frameworks across $N = 144$ SMEs, Ahmad, Abu Bakar & Ahmad (2019) found that SME social media adoption had **no statistically significant impact on business performance ($\beta = 0.098, p > 0.05$)**. They attribute this failure to a *"bandwagon effect"* ($\beta = 0.304, p < 0.01$): small businesses establish social media pages reactively because competitors do so, accumulating superficial visibility ("likes" and followers) without a clear conversion strategy.

Resolving this contradiction, Roosdhani, Wibowo & Widiastuti (2023) ($N = 109$ SMEs) demonstrated via Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS-SEM) that social media marketing enhances SME sales performance **primarily when mediated by new customer acquisition ($\beta = 0.720, p < 0.001$)** and brand equity ($\beta = 0.723, p < 0.001$), rather than through direct unmediated activity. Despite the centrality of customer acquisition to SME survival, Azhar & Juidette’s (2025) PRISMA systematic review of 263 scholarly records published between 2015 and 2024 revealed that **only 12 studies globally** have investigated digital marketing specifically for **customer acquisition** (as distinct from brand awareness or customer retention), with only **one qualitative study** examining SMEs and **one single-channel study** linking social media engagement to acquisition. Furthermore, in an investigation of Indian small businesses, De (2025) demonstrated that emerging-market micro and small enterprises rarely convert first-time buyers through automated website checkouts alone; instead, they rely on public social media (Instagram and Facebook reels/ads) for upper-funnel discovery and depend on **direct conversational touchpoints—specifically WhatsApp Business catalogs, one-on-one chat, status updates, and UPI payment links—to establish personal trust and close the first sale**. Because Ahmad et al. (2019), Roosdhani et al. (2023), and De (2025) all examine the phenomenon from the **business owner’s perspective**, the literature lacks customer-level empirical evidence explaining which digital touchpoints actually persuade a consumer to make their first purchase from a small business.

#### 2.2 Digital Touchpoint Taxonomies and Multi-Stage Customer Journeys
From the consumer's perspective, acquiring a product or service involves interacting with multiple digital touchpoints across pre-purchase, purchase, and post-purchase stages (Baxendale et al., 2015; Herhausen et al., 2019; Moliner & Tortosa-Edo, 2024). Rather than treating digital touchpoints as a single undifferentiated construct, customer journey scholarship classifies touchpoints along two complementary dimensions: **functional role** and **source ownership/control**. Using Latent Class Analysis (LCA) across $N = 2,348$ consumers evaluating nine digital touchpoints, Hallikainen, Alamäki & Laukkanen (2019) classified digital touchpoints into **Functional Touchpoints** (search engines, brand websites, email, and live chat), **Social Touchpoints** (social networking sites, photo-sharing communities such as Instagram, and video communities such as YouTube), and **Community Touchpoints** (blogs and discussion forums). Crucially, Hallikainen et al. (2019) found that across all four consumer segments, **functional touchpoints—specifically search engines ($M = 3.98$) and brand websites ($M = 3.86$)—were rated significantly more important for actual purchasing decisions than social media touchpoints ($M = 1.98\text{–}2.46$)**, which primarily supported initial inspiration.

Complementing this functional taxonomy, Moliner & Tortosa-Edo (2024) ($N = 450$) and Herhausen et al. (2019) ($N = 7,392$ journeys across 17 touchpoints) distinguish **External (Brand-Owned) Touchpoints** from **Internal (Independent/Peer-Generated) Touchpoints**. Moliner & Tortosa-Edo (2024) proved that independent peer touchpoints (such as online customer reviews and forums) exert a stronger influence on both cognitive experience ($\beta = 0.431\text{–}0.485$) and affective experience than brand-controlled touchpoints across the customer journey. Methodologically, in a real-time experience tracking study of $N = 13,962$ customer journeys, Baxendale, Macdonald & Wilson (2015) established that measuring **touchpoint positivity (`AvgPos`—the perceived affective quality/valence of each interaction)** alongside **touchpoint exposure frequency ($\ln(\text{Freq}+1)$)** nearly doubles the explained variance in brand consideration. Furthermore, across a lab experiment ($N = 10,470$) and a field experiment on Amazon, Bleier, Harmeling & Palmatier (2019) demonstrated how 13 specific online touchpoint elements shape four experience dimensions (`Informativeness`, `Entertainment`, `Social Presence`, and `Sensory Appeal`), proving a vital boundary condition for small businesses: **less-familiar, low-trustworthiness brands achieve substantially greater sales conversion lifts from social presence (e.g., conversational linguistic style, user reviews) and informative cues** because first-time buyers face heightened uncertainty. Nevertheless, existing multi-touchpoint journey studies either stop at brand consideration (Baxendale et al., 2015) and e-satisfaction (Moliner & Tortosa-Edo, 2024) or focus on repeat-purchase loyalty at large established retailers (Herhausen et al., 2019).

#### 2.3 The Psychological Conversion Mechanism: Digital Engagement, Brand Trust, eWOM, and Perceived Value
To explain *how* digital touchpoint encounters move a consumer toward a purchase decision, marketing theory posits a sequential cognitive–affective–behavioral progression. Grounded in Customer Engagement Theory and the Elaboration Likelihood Model, Hollebeek & Macky (2019) conceptualized a three-tier hierarchy in which functional and hedonic digital interactions first stimulate **Cognitive, Emotional, and Behavioral Customer Engagement** (Tier 1), which subsequently builds **Brand Trust—encompassing both Credibility and Benevolence** (Tier 2)—and ultimately enhances **Consumer Perceived Value** (Tier 3).

Empirical studies across social commerce and electronic word-of-mouth (eWOM) strongly support this hierarchy while revealing sharp differences in how specific touchpoints trigger Engagement versus Trust and Value. In a Covariance-Based SEM (CB-SEM) study of **$N = 625$ Indian consumers** across WhatsApp, Instagram, Facebook, and YouTube, George, Joseph, Abraham & Joseph (2023) found that while **social media likes and shares** strongly drive **Brand Engagement ($\beta = 0.47, p < 0.001$)**, they exert the weakest effect on **Brand Trust ($\beta = 0.25$)**; conversely, **Social Presence of Interaction (one-on-one chat/messaging)** ($\beta = 0.33$ on Trust; $\beta = 0.48$ on Engagement) and **Online Reviews and Ratings** ($\beta = 0.32$ on Trust; $\beta = 0.42$ on Engagement) are critical to establish consumer trust. Similarly, applying the Information Adoption Model (IAM) across $N = 384$ consumers and $N = 10$ interviews to compare eWOM on **Social Media** versus **Shopping Websites**, Erkan & Evans (2018) proved that **shopping website reviews exert a strong positive effect on Online Purchase Intention ($\beta = 0.358\text{–}0.430, p < 0.001$) via Information Quality, Information Credibility, and Perceived Usefulness, whereas peer posts on social media have no significant direct effect on purchase intention ($\beta \approx 0$)**. Corroborating these findings at a global scale, Ismagilova et al.’s (2020) quantitative meta-analysis of **69 empirical studies** ($N$ up to $29,593$) established that **eWOM Volume alone is not a statistically significant predictor of intention to buy ($r = 0.245, p = 0.068$)**, whereas **Trust in the Message ($r = 0.506, p < 0.001$)**, **eWOM Usefulness ($r = 0.496, p < 0.001$)**, and **Argument Quality ($r = 0.449, p < 0.001$)** are powerful determinants of purchase intention. Together, these studies prove that high engagement volume on social media cannot drive conversion unless supported by credibility-building touchpoints (reviews, websites, and conversational chat) that create **Trust** and **Perceived Value/Usefulness**.

#### 2.4 Bridging the Intention–Behavior Gap: From Purchase Intention to Actual First Purchase
A pervasive limitation across consumer digital marketing research—including Erkan & Evans (2018), Ismagilova et al. (2020), and George et al. (2023)—is stopping at **Purchase Intention** rather than measuring **Actual Purchase / Customer Acquisition**. Synthesizing **68 empirical studies** via Meta-Analytic Structural Equation Modeling (MASEM), Dwivedi et al. (2023) proved the existence of a substantial **Intention–Behavior Gap** in digital and social commerce: while antecedent constructs explain **$48.5\%$ of the variance in Behavioral Intention ($R^2 = 0.485$)**, the structural path from **Behavioral Intention to Actual Purchase Behavior is only $\beta = 0.28$ ($p < 0.01$)**, accounting for **$29.2\%$ of the variance in Actual Behavior ($R^2 = 0.292$)**. Crucially, Dwivedi et al.’s (2023) modified MASEM uncovered an **emergent direct conversion pathway**: **Trust ($\beta = 0.33, p < 0.01$)**—an effect exceeding that of behavioral intention itself—and **Emotional Support ($\beta = 0.16, p < 0.01$)** directly drive **Actual Purchase Behavior**. When purchasing from an unfamiliar small seller for the first time, consumers with high purchase intention still abandon transactions at checkout unless sufficient **Trust** and **Perceived Value** are present to overcome payment and fulfillment risk.

Methodologically, Kim, Ferrin & Rao (2008) established the foundational two-stage empirical design for modeling both psychological intention and actual binary conversion. Across a two-wave study ($N = 468$ in Wave 1; $N = 261$ actual buyers coded $Y = 1$ vs. $N = 207$ non-buyers coded $Y = 0$ in Wave 2), Kim et al. (2008) combined **PLS-SEM** with **Binary Logistic Regression**, proving that `Information Quality` ($\beta = 0.265$) and `Perceived Reputation` ($\beta = 0.289$) increase `Consumer Trust` and reduce `Perceived Risk`, which alongside `Perceived Benefit (Value)` ($\beta = 0.355, p < 0.001$) drive `Purchase Intention` ($R^2 = 0.339$) and significantly predict **Actual Binary Purchase ($Y \in \{0,1\}$, Nagelkerke $R^2 = 0.301$, Wald $= 67.32, p < 0.001$)**.

#### 2.5 Multi-Touch Marketing Attribution and Explainable Conversion Analytics
While psychological SEM studies explain *why* consumers form intentions to buy, multi-touch attribution (MTA) models in marketing analytics seek to quantify *which* digital touchpoints deserve credit for an actual conversion ($Y \in \{0,1\}$). In a comprehensive synthesis of **62 marketing attribution studies**, Buhalis & Volchek (2021) developed a five-dimensional taxonomy of attribution models—spanning heuristic rules (first-touch, last-touch), statistical regressions (Binary Logistic Regression, survival models), Markov graphs, and cooperative game-theoretic / machine learning methods (**Shapley Values / SHAP** and neural networks)—and demonstrated that single-touch heuristics severely misallocate marketing credit in multi-touchpoint journeys. Extending this analytical stream, Du, Zhong, Nair, Cui & Shou (2019) formulated how **Shapley Value attribution (SHAP)**, **Logistic Regression**, and **Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs)** quantify each digital channel’s marginal contribution to binary conversion ($Y \in \{0,1\}$) across nine digital touchpoints, while clarifying the boundary between predictive Shapley attribution and counterfactual incrementality.

However, Buhalis & Volchek (2021, pp. 31–39) identified a fundamental methodological gap in the attribution literature: **algorithmic multi-touch attribution models rely exclusively on server-side clickstream exposure logs (`Click → Conversion`) that small businesses do not possess across fragmented platforms (such as Instagram reels moving to WhatsApp chat), and they treat the customer's mind as a "black box," ignoring the psychological state transitions (`Digital Engagement → Brand Trust → Perceived Value`) that actually produce conversion**.

#### 2.6 Summary of the Research Gap
Synthesizing the five streams of literature reveals a clear, three-part research gap:
1. **Context & Perspective Gap:** Only 12 studies globally examine digital marketing for customer acquisition (Azhar & Juidette, 2025), and existing SME studies rely on firm-owner self-reports (Ahmad et al., 2019; Roosdhani et al., 2023) rather than customer-level journey evidence.
2. **Touchpoint & Outcome Gap:** Prior consumer studies either isolate a single channel and stop at **Purchase Intention** (Erkan & Evans, 2018; Ismagilova et al., 2020; George et al., 2023)—ignoring the **Intention–Behavior Gap ($\beta = 0.28$)** proved by Dwivedi et al. (2023)—or examine repeat-purchase loyalty at large Western retailers (Herhausen et al., 2019) while omitting the **Direct Conversational Touchpoints (WhatsApp Business / Instagram DMs)** upon which small businesses depend (De, 2025).
3. **Methodological / Attribution Gap:** Algorithmic attribution models (Du et al., 2019) count clicks without psychological mediators (Buhalis & Volchek, 2021), whereas psychological SEM models rarely attribute dichotomous **Actual First Purchase ($Y \in \{0,1\}$)** across competing touchpoints (Kim, Ferrin & Rao, 2008).

To address this gap, the present study integrates **PLS-SEM** (modeling how **Social Media**, **Search/Website**, **Earned Review/eWOM**, and **Direct Conversational Touchpoints** drive **Digital Engagement**, **Brand Trust**, **Perceived Value**, and **Purchase Intention**) with **Explainable Multi-Touch Conversion Attribution (`Binary Logistic Regression / ML + Shapley Value [SHAP] Attribution`)** to predict **Actual First-Time Customer Acquisition ($Y \in \{0,1\}$)** in small businesses.
